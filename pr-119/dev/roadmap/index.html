    Roadmap

    Alpha phase

    Alto is in alpha. Complete means the feature is available in the current development version. Planned marks the proposed development sequence. Under consideration marks a candidate for future work.

    Chord construction and staff-to-keyboard practice are next. Ear training and rhythm come next. Both provide the groundwork for melodic dictation and harmonic analysis.

    Notation and pitch

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Note identification and construction Name a written note or place a named pitch in the requested octave and clef. Complete
    Clefs Identify a clef or place its symbol on the correct staff line. Complete
    Notation symbols Identify a highlighted symbol in a short excerpt, or find a named symbol on the staff. Complete
    Accidental scope Work out which pitch sounds after a cancellation or a barline. Under consideration
    Enharmonic spelling Respell a pitch to fit a specified key or harmonic role. Under consideration

    Intervals and instruments

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Staff intervals Identify written seconds through fifteenths, shown together or in ascending or descending order. Complete
    Keyboard notes and intervals Name a highlighted piano key or identify the interval between two highlighted keys. Complete
    Interval construction Place the second note a named interval above or below the first. Feedback distinguishes an incorrect staff position from an incorrect accidental. Complete
    Staff-to-keyboard practice Select the piano key for a written note, then play scales and select chord tones on the keyboard. Planned
    Transposition Rewrite a phrase in a new key or convert a transposing instrument’s part to concert pitch. Planned
    Interval inversion and reduction Invert a minor sixth or reduce a major tenth to its simple interval. Under consideration
    Instrument ranges and fingerboards Find an out-of-range note in a part or locate a pitch on a fretboard in a given tuning. Under consideration
    MIDI performance Play a written phrase on a connected keyboard. Under consideration

    Scales and tonality

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Scale identification and construction Identify the key or mode of a written scale, or build the scale on the staff. Complete
    Key signatures Identify and construct signatures and fill in missing sharps or flats in order. Complete
    Written scale degrees Read a note as a scale degree or place a requested degree on the staff in a given key. Complete
    Relative keys Find the relative major or minor on the circle of fifths. Complete
    Parallel keys Compare scales that share a tonic but have different key signatures. Under consideration
    Other scale families Build and compare pentatonic, blues, whole-tone, and octatonic scales. Under consideration

    Ear training

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Intervals by ear Name an interval after hearing its notes in succession or together. Replay the example and see the notation after answering. Planned
    Chords by ear Identify root-position triads by sound, then identify seventh chords and inversions. Planned
    Tonal scale degrees Hear a key established, then identify the scale degree of a new pitch. Planned
    Pitch recall Reproduce a sequence of two or three pitches. Planned
    Scales and tonal centers by ear Identify a heard scale or find the tonic of a short phrase. Under consideration
    Singing and intonation Sing a requested degree, continue a phrase, or judge a pitch against a reference. Under consideration
    Tuning and acoustics Match frequency ratios, identify harmonic partials, or listen for beats between pitches. Under consideration

    Rhythm and meter

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Durations, rests, dots, and ties Complete a measure or replace tied notes with an equivalent duration. Planned
    Meter and grouping Subdivide beats and correct the beaming or rest grouping in simple and compound meters. Planned
    Tuplets Fill a beat with a triplet or another specified division. Planned
    Rhythm reading and imitation Tap a steady pulse, a written rhythm, or a pattern heard after a count-in. Calibrated timing distinguishes missing or extra taps from tempo drift. Planned
    Rhythm dictation Match a heard rhythm to written examples, then enter the pattern in the given meter. Planned
    Syncopation and groove Locate a displaced accent or distinguish straight and swung subdivisions by ear. Under consideration
    Additive meter, polyrhythm, and rhythmic cycles Group a measure of 7/8, align three pulses against two, or identify the return of a taught cycle. Under consideration

    Chords and functional harmony

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Chord quality Identify root-position triads and seventh chords on the staff. Complete
    Arpeggio inversions Name the chord type and inversion from its notes written in succession. Complete
    Roman numerals on the circle of fifths Name the highlighted chord by Roman numeral in a major key. Complete
    Chord construction Spell a named triad or seventh chord by placing its notes on the staff. Planned
    Block-chord inversions Identify the root, bass, and inversion when the chord tones are written together. Planned
    Diatonic function and minor-key harmony Label or build chords in a major or minor key, including chords that use a raised degree in minor. Planned
    Cadences and progressions Identify a cadence or analyze a short progression across different voicings. Planned
    Harmonic rhythm Mark where the harmony changes within a phrase. Under consideration
    Chromatic harmony and modulation Resolve an applied dominant, identify a borrowed or altered chord, or locate a pivot between keys. Under consideration
    Jazz and popular harmony Read lead-sheet symbols, identify guide tones, build voicings, and analyze blues progressions or repeating chord patterns. Under consideration

    Melody, counterpoint, and form

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Melodic dictation Write a heard phrase, with separate feedback on pitch and rhythm. Planned
    Two-voice dictation Transcribe two independent lines from the same excerpt. Under consideration
    Contour and motif Trace a melody’s shape, find a recurring motif, or transform it by inversion, retrograde, or augmentation. Under consideration
    Nonchord tones and voice leading Identify a passing tone or suspension, or correct parallel fifths, voice crossing, or an unresolved tendency tone. Under consideration
    Counterpoint and four-part writing Add a line to a given melody or realize a figured bass in four voices. Under consideration
    Phrase structure and form Distinguish a period from a sentence or mark the sections of a piece. Under consideration
    Texture and orchestration Identify a texture in an excerpt or choose a playable instrumental doubling. Under consideration
    Post-tonal theory Find a pitch-class set’s prime form or identify a transformation of a twelve-tone row. Under consideration
    Modal traditions Identify a characteristic phrase or mode segment from examples within a specific tradition. Under consideration
    Composition and improvisation Write or play a short response that meets a given musical constraint. Under consideration

    Lessons and practice

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Guided lessons Study pitches and notes, clefs, intervals, scales, key signatures, and the circle of fifths, then open related exercises. Complete
    Spaced repetition and statistics Review questions under the selected scheduler and inspect accuracy, response times, and individual answers in a local profile. Complete
    Response-time grading Inspect response times by exercise and adjust the percentiles used for automatic SM-2 and FSRS grades. Complete
    Mixed practice Combine an earlier prerequisite, a current topic, and a passage that uses both in one suggested session. Planned

    App features

    Feature What the learner does Status
    Anki-compatible deck export Export filtered exercise questions and optional review schedules for practice in Anki. Complete
    Instrument packs Install third-party artwork, samples, and exercises for identifying fingerboard notes or marked instrument parts from one ZIP. Plugins · Viola plugin tutorial. Complete
    Instrument plugins Add an instrument with its own sound and play it using an interactive on-screen version of the instrument. Planned
    Exercise and lesson plugins Install custom exercises and lessons and use them alongside Alto’s built-in material. Planned
    Glossary plugins Install a plugin that adds new terms to the glossary, with definitions, interactive examples, and links to related lessons and exercises. Planned

    Beta phase

    Alto is developed with the help of artificial intelligence. The main goal for beta is to replace prose generated by large language models with human-written text throughout the app and its documentation. Lessons, exercise prompts, and feedback should sound like a teacher explaining an idea to a student. The editorial review will also check definitions, musical examples, and answers for accuracy and correct errors where needed.

    A second goal is to refactor the codebase so it is easier to read, test, and maintain. That work includes simplifying complicated code, removing duplication, and using tests to verify behavior as the implementation changes.
