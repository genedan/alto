Lessons

Lessons are step-by-step walks through a topic for someone meeting it for the first time. Nothing about them is scored or recorded.

Start with Pitches and Notes to connect a sound to a piano key and a written note. Its last step opens Keyboard Note Identification, Note Identification, Note Construction, or Notation Identification. Each exercise’s book button opens hints for that exercise and a link back to the lesson. Clef Basics then explains how to read different clefs. Intervals builds on staff reading to name the distance between two pitches. Scale Identification uses that notation to explore patterns of notes. Key Signatures teaches how to read and write their sharps and flats. The Circle of Fifths shows how keys and their signatures relate.

When you open an exercise from a lesson, Back to lesson at the upper left of the exercise returns you to the step you left.

Finding them

The home page has a switch at the top, Exercises and Lessons. Choose Lessons to see a tile for each lesson, with the icon of the exercise that practices the same topic: the Circle of Fifths lesson has the circle of fifths icon. Alto remembers which side you were on. The cards change with the transition chosen under Settings… › Appearance › Transitions. Use Tiles to open the catalog for the selected section.

During an exercise or lesson, the two icons switch the navigation list. Your current activity stays open until you choose another one.

Once you have begun a lesson, a bar along the bottom of its tile shows how far through you are and the corner says Step N/M. Click it to open the lesson, and Leave lesson (or Esc) to return to where you were. Start over goes back to the first step.

The Circle of Fifths exercise also has a way in: open its reference pane with the book button, and press New to this? Take the full lesson at the top. The Clef Basics exercise has the same, with a table of the clefs under the button.

The lesson list

Inside a lesson, the list on the left shows the lessons, as the exercise list does in an exercise. The lesson that is open is in bold, with its steps listed under it and the one you are on highlighted. Click a step to go to it, or another lesson to open that. The button at the top left of the lesson hides and shows the list, and Alto remembers your choice.

Inside a lesson

A lesson is a series of steps. The title and progress are at the top and Back and Next are at the bottom. Back on the first step, and Finish on the last, leave the lesson.

A step has some reading, and may have:

  • A sound to hear. A button plays the notes being described, in the instrument chosen in the settings.
  • A keyboard. Pitches and Notes starts with a bracket over two black keys and a C on the white key just to their left. The labels stay in place while the first two notes play and when you identify C’s neighbor. When you play a keyboard example, follow the keys as each note sounds. Chord tones light together. The original highlights return when playback stops.
  • Staves. The clef lesson shows staves beside the reading, each with a clef, sometimes a note, and a line under it. In a small exercise the staff changes with the question.
  • The circle of fifths. Click a key, or use the arrow keys, to see its key signature and its chords. Some steps have Down a fifth and Up a fifth buttons that walk around the circle for you.
  • A small exercise. It cannot be failed. A wrong answer gets a hint, a second wrong answer shows the answer with its explanation, and a right answer is explained. Try again repeats a single question. In checks with multiple questions, Try another draws a question for the next attempt.

Intervals

Allow about 12–15 minutes for a first pass. Count letter names to find an interval’s number, then count half steps to determine its quality. The staff and keyboard illustrations use the pitches played by the listening buttons. The lesson compares C–E with C–E♭, introduces perfect intervals, and shows why C–F♯ and C–G♭ use the same two piano keys. A final check asks you to name an unfamiliar pair.

The last step links to Interval Identification for reading two written notes, Keyboard Interval Identification for naming the interval between two piano keys, and Interval Construction for placing the second note on the staff. Each exercise has a book button with hints and a link to the lesson.

Key Signatures

Allow about 10–15 minutes. Start with two ways to write the same F♯. Listen to what happens when a natural cancels the signature, then read sharp and flat signatures to find major keys and their relative minors. Choose the staff you would write for A♭ major and try a fresh signature in treble or bass clef.

The closing step links to Key Signature Identification, Key Signature Construction, and Order of Keys. Choose Order of Keys to place the individual sharps or flats in order. The book button in each exercise opens hints and a link back to the lesson.

The Circle of Fifths lesson

Eight steps for a beginner:

  1. A map of major keys. What a key is, and what a key signature is.
  2. A fifth. Counting five letter names, and hearing the interval. A small exercise: name the letter a fifth above.
  3. Fifths clockwise from C. Each step clockwise adds a sharp.
  4. Sharp keys on the circle. Find the key with a given number of sharps.
  5. Flat keys on the circle. Counterclockwise adds flats; where the two meet. Find the key with a given number of flats.
  6. Relative minors on the inner ring. Find the relative minor of a key.
  7. A key and its neighbors. The IV and V of a key. Find the key a fifth above or below another.
  8. Circle of fifths practice. A summary, and a button to practice in the Circle of Fifths exercise.

The Clef Basics lesson

Ten steps for a beginner:

  1. Pitch and the clef. The same place on the staff is a different note in the treble and bass clefs.
  2. The three clef symbols. Every clef is the G clef, the C clef or the F clef, put on a line.
  3. The G clef. The treble clef, and the French violin clef on the bottom line.
  4. The F clef. The bass clef on the fourth line, the subbass clef on the top line, and the baritone clef.
  5. The C clef and middle C. Soprano, mezzo-soprano, alto and tenor are the C clef on four lines, and each centers on middle C.
  6. The rarer clefs. French violin, subbass and the two ways to write the baritone clef.
  7. The clef symbol. A clef is drawn: is it the G, C or F clef?
  8. The clef’s line. A named clef: which line of a blank staff is it written on?
  9. The clef’s name. The two halves together, as in Clef Identification.
  10. Clef reading and placement. A summary, and a button to practice in the Clef Basics exercise.
