Instrument samples: University of Iowa Electronic Music Studios, Musical Instrument Samples
(https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MIS.html). The source states the recordings are freely
available and may be used for any project, without restrictions.

Used: marimba (yarn mallets, ff), vibraphone (sustain, ff), the viola, flute and French horn (see below).
Converted to mono 22.05 kHz, silence trimmed from the start, cut to 2.2 s with a fade-out and levelled
per instrument. For the marimba and vibraphone only every second semitone is stored (file names are MIDI
note numbers); the app pitch-shifts the nearest stored note for the others.

Viola (viola_bowed and viola_plucked): University of Iowa Electronic Music Studios, Musical Instrument Samples,
Viola (2012) (https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MISviola2012.html). Played by Manuel Tabora on a viola by Anton Krutz
(2001), recorded 11 May 2012 in an anechoic chamber with Earthworks QTC40 microphones. The mono files for arco
(bowed) and pizz (plucked) were used. Same terms as above.

The headers of these files say 44.1 kHz, but the audio is 96 kHz: read at 44.1 kHz every note sounds 13 semitones low and
2.2 times too slow. They were read at 96 kHz, which gives the expected pitches. Each file is a chromatic run on one
string. The notes were found one by one and matched to the run in order, then checked against their own harmonics.
Processing as for the other instruments (mono 22.05 kHz, 10 ms before the onset, levelled to an RMS of 0.11, peaks held
under 0.9).

viola_bowed: mezzo-forte (mf), 45 notes, every semitone from C3 to A6 (MIDI 48 to 93) except D#3, which failed the
harmonic check; Alto pitch-shifts a neighbour for that one. Each note is cut to 2.2 s with a 0.4 s fade-out.
  C string C3-D3 and E3-A3, G string A#3-B3 (G3-B3 run) and C4-C#4 (C4-B4 run), D string D4-B4 and C5-B5,
  A string C6-A6.
  Because every semitone is stored, Alto does not have to borrow a neighbouring note, which would change the tone.

viola_plucked: fortissimo (ff), 20 notes, every second semitone from C3 to F#6 (MIDI 48 to 90). Notes are cut where the
sound has died away (0.6 to 1.6 s) with a short fade-out.
  C string C3-B3 and C4, D string D4-B4, A string C5-B5 and C6-F#6.
  Left out: F#5, whose take carries a loud G5 ring, and D6 and everything above F#6, which are too weak to identify
  reliably. The app pitch-shifts the nearest stored note for those and for the odd semitones. The G#5 and A#5
  plucks carry some sympathetic ringing from the recording.

Flute (flute): Flute (2000), non-vibrato set (https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MIS-Pitches-2012/MISFlute2012.html). Played by
Sonja Feig, April 2000, anechoic chamber, Neumann KM 84 microphone; 16-bit 44.1 kHz. Fortissimo, no vibrato, the stereo
note files (Flute.nonvib.ff.<note>.stereo.aiff) from the site's zip, one per semitone. Same terms as above.
39 notes, every semitone from B3 to C#7 (MIDI 59 to 97). Mixed down to mono, 22.05 kHz, 10 ms before the onset, cut to 2.2 s
with a 0.4 s fade-out (the shortest notes, which end earlier, fade over their last 0.4 s), levelled to an RMS of 0.11 and
peaks held under 0.9. Every file's fundamental was checked against its name. The recording is not perfectly in tune: the
highest notes are up to about 45 cents sharp. This is left as recorded.

French horn (horn): French Horn (1997) (https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MIS-Pitches-2012/MISHorn2012.html). Played by
Jessica Wiskus, 27 July 1997, anechoic chamber, Neumann KM 84 microphone, 5 feet away; 16-bit 44.1 kHz. Fortissimo, the stereo
note files (Horn.ff.<note>.stereo.aiff) from the site's zip, one per semitone. Same terms as above.
44 notes, every semitone from A#1 to F5 (MIDI 34 to 77). Processed as the flute. Every file's fundamental was checked
against its name. The lowest notes are up to about 45 cents flat. This is left as recorded.
